About High Blood Pressure

Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
Reviewed page: https://www.cdc.gov/high-blood-pressure/about/
Reviewed: 2026-08-31

This is a concise educational source card, not a reproduction of the CDC page and not medical advice. High blood pressure, also called hypertension, is consistently at or above 130/80 mm Hg according to the CDC page reviewed. It often has no warning signs or symptoms. Measurement and a health care professional's assessment are needed to determine whether a person has high blood pressure and to make treatment decisions.

Over time, high blood pressure can affect the heart, brain, kidneys, and eyes. CDC describes regular physical activity, avoiding smoking, a healthy eating pattern that limits sodium and alcohol, healthy weight, and stress management as measures that can support a healthy range. Some people also need prescribed medicine.
