Diabetes Basics

Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
Reviewed page: https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/about/
Reviewed: 2026-08-31

This is a concise educational source card, not a reproduction of the CDC page and not medical advice. Diabetes is a long-term condition involving the way the body uses glucose for energy. In type 1 diabetes, an autoimmune process damages insulin-producing cells, so daily insulin is needed. In type 2 diabetes, the body does not use insulin effectively and blood glucose can rise over time. Gestational diabetes occurs during pregnancy and can increase later type 2 diabetes risk.

Diabetes can contribute to serious complications. Diabetes management and diagnosis require a health care professional. CDC describes healthy eating, physical activity, prescribed medicines, diabetes education, and follow-up care as relevant parts of management. Lifestyle changes can help prevent or delay type 2 diabetes and prediabetes; they do not prevent type 1 diabetes.
