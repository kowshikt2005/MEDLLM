Symptoms of Diabetes

Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
Reviewed page: https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/signs-symptoms/index.html
Reviewed: 2026-08-31

This is a concise educational source card, not a reproduction of the CDC page and not a diagnostic tool. Type 1 and type 2 diabetes can begin with few or no symptoms. Symptoms described by CDC include frequent urination, increased thirst or hunger, unplanned weight loss, fatigue, mood changes, blurred vision, and frequent urinary tract or yeast infections.

Type 1 symptoms can appear quickly and may include nausea, vomiting, stomach pain, or diabetic ketoacidosis. Type 2 symptoms can take years to develop; slow-healing wounds, darker skin patches, and numbness or tingling are among additional possible signs. Gestational diabetes commonly has no symptoms and is assessed during pregnancy by a health care professional.

Symptoms alone cannot establish a diagnosis. Read the current CDC source directly because the page may be updated.
